I’ll Regret Not Rebelling for the Rest of My Life

I’m getting more and more anxious about my age. I can hardly find anyone who started HRT later than I did. All I used to dream about was getting a degree, getting good grades, and living up to my family’s hopes. The hardest part was that my parents banned me from using any electronic devices before the gaokao, the college entrance exam. I went to boarding school and barely got any time off. But did I really have no chance at all to start exploring my gender?

I did have a chance, actually. I just didn’t dare rebel. I was afraid my parents would beat me. I was afraid of dropping out and having to work. Even in my second year of high school, when I knew I was depressed, I didn’t dare flee to another city like Su Wanqing did. All I dared do was run away for a day. Then I realized I had no phone and no money for a ticket or a meal, so I had to slink back home. My parents mocked me for it: “Look at you, all grown up. You’ve learned to run away from home.”

I used to cling to the idea that I could prove myself through my grades and earn my parents’ love. In the end, my teachers and parents turned me into a machine that churned out answers to exam questions. I can’t say they deserve no credit, but was a degree from Jiaotong University really what I wanted? No! I’d sacrifice that degree to have started HRT in high school. Even a job offer worth a million after graduation wouldn’t make me happy, because the youth I could have had as a girl is gone. Gone forever. I wanted to be like xyn on Twitter, like Su Yuqing: drop out, leave home, earn my own money, and start HRT early.

The point of life shouldn’t be degrees, grades, or money. It should be to make yourself happy. For trans people, the best life would be one where you start HRT before puberty.

I made the wrong choice. This is how the world is punishing me.

My parents pushed me into it, but I could have had it out with them. The only reason I didn’t was that I wanted to do a little better on the gaokao. I destroyed Su Yuqing’s alternate timeline with my own hands.

These are the wrong choices that will stay nailed to the past: when I could get into high school by recommendation, I picked one close to home and didn’t get away from my family; I secretly bought a tablet in high school, but didn’t dare lose myself online; and when I was depressed, I didn’t kill myself or hurt myself because I didn’t want the school to pressure me into leaving.

This time, I found the courage to make the right choice. But it’s too late for everything…
